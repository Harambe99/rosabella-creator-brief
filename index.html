01
Borrowed authority

Green-screen a credible clip

React to a doctor, researcher or news segment on the ingredient. The authority convinces; you bridge to the product.

“A dermatologist just explained why glutathione does this to your skin…”
02
Self-recognition

Name the problem first

Open on the exact thing the viewer sees in the mirror. Specific feels personal — that is what stops the scroll.

“If your skin looks dull like this by 2pm, here’s why…”
03
Buyer protection

What to check before you buy

Teach viewers how to judge the category, then show the product passing the test you just set.

“Before you buy any glutathione, check this one thing on the label.”
04
Routine in context

Where it sits in your day

Show it inside a real routine, not alone on a counter. Cheapest to film and a consistently steady performer.

“My 6am stack, in the order I actually take it.”
04
Non-negotiable

The rules

Do

  • Stick to the claims on the labelIf it is not on the bottle or written here, do not say it.
  • Say that results varyWhenever you show a personal result.
  • Use supportive language“Supports”, “may help”. Possibility, never promise.
  • Tag it as a paid promotionUse TikTok’s own disclosure toggle.

Don’t

  • Never name a disease or conditionNo treating, curing or managing anything.
  • Never present as a doctorUnless you are licensed.
  • No guaranteed or instant resultsNothing “will” happen. No overnight claims.
  • Do not invent specsNo dosages or ingredients that are not on the label.

Language that gets videos taken down

These are supplements, not medicine. Anything that implies treating, curing or managing a condition risks the video and your account — and any spend already behind it. Avoid these entirely:

lowers blood pressure cures treats reverses prevents clinically proven better than medication doctor recommended FDA approved detoxes your liver guaranteed results
